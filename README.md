An estuary is where two waters meet and neither fully wins. That felt
like the right shape for this day, so this page is built like one:
a bronze-and-blue front page where the whole of Tuesday 25 November
2026 flows into a single 1024×1024 sheet — thirty-seven stories, every
one of them dated that day, every one of them sourced.

The heaviest water runs across the top. A new count puts Russian war
dead above 700,000, and the page gives that figure the wide scene it
deserves: a field of crosses under a dark sky. Beside it, Iran's claim
that three tankers were mined in the Strait of Hormuz — printed as a
claim, in a red CLAIMED tag, because that is all it is — and NATO's
early-warning jets bound for Poland, drawn as an AWACS turning over
its own radar rings.

Below the leads, ten more scenes carry the day in smaller frames:
Baghdad moving on the Kurdish oilfields, the Gaza drone strike that
killed seven, Brussels tightening sanctions again, the first German
Lynx vehicles reaching Ukraine, fifty Boeing jets ordered from Xiamen,
Madrid's 5–1 rout of PSV, twelve coral species coaxed back from the
edge, Washington waiting on a PCE number that had not landed yet (the
card prints no figure, because there wasn't one), Nvidia's trillion-
dollar Monday, and the storms queuing up over both American coasts
ahead of Thanksgiving.

Around the stories sits the day's furniture, all of it checked
against the calendar: an eight-figure number strip, a region strip
that counts its own stories honestly, twenty-four further briefs in
number order, a six-line timeline, a dotted map pinned with the
thirteen full cards, and a DATE CHECK box that names five tempting
stories and refuses them — a game launch that is only a date, climate
talks from earlier in the month, a weekend football score, two-day-old
Mercury explainers, and a Black Friday that belongs to Friday.

Two layouts of this page were drawn and compared; the one in
`entry.png` is the survivor. `compose_b.py` renders it from the day's
locked story pool, `compose_a.py` keeps the losing layout for the
record, and `main.py` does the humble work of the submission itself:
it copies `entry.png` to `/output/entry.png`, byte for byte, using
nothing beyond the Python standard library.

The reporting came through RFE/RL, Al Jazeera, DW, Forbes and Starlit
over the wires and wire roundups; where a number is a claim, the page
says so on its face. The drawings are originals, made for this page.
